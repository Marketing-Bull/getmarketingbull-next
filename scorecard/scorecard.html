Qualification

C

Answering fast only helps if the call ends with the right next step. A consistent script protects the caller and the case: it gets contact details before the line can drop, gathers what an attorney needs to evaluate the case, and leaves a qualified caller with a booked consult or a retainer, not a promise of a callback.

Tip: listen to how your intake team opens a call. "Are you somewhere safe? Are you hurt?" followed by "Let me take your name and number in case we get cut off" covers questions 10 and 11 in the first minute.

ExampleWhat good looks like

Every call follows the same order, and every call ends with something concrete. A qualified caller leaves with a consult on the calendar or an e-sign retainer in their inbox the same day. A caller you can't help is referred to a firm that can, and the referral is logged, so the lead is not simply lost.

The PI Intake Scorecard07

What to fix first

Start with your lowest section

You don't need new software to fix most of these. Start where you scored lowest, and pick the changes your team can make this month.

ACoverage

  • Give new clients a dedicated number that rings straight to intake, not the front desk.
  • Cover nights and weekends with a live, trained answer that follows your script: staff on call, an on-call attorney, or an answering service or voice agent that takes the intake and books the callback.
  • Rule for everyone who answers: take the name and number before any hold or transfer.
  • Arrange Spanish-language intake, including after hours.

BSpeed to lead

  • Send web-form and chat alerts to a phone as a text, not only to an inbox.
  • Name one person who owns the missed-call queue, with a five-minute callback target.
  • Turn on a missed-call text-back that tells the caller a person is calling them back.
  • Report response time from timestamps every month, not from memory.

CQualification

  • Write the script down in order: safety and injury, contact details and permission to text, accident, injuries, treatment, insurance, prior representation.
  • Let intake send an e-sign retainer or book the consult on the call, without waiting for an attorney.
  • Keep a referral list for cases you don't take, and log every referral.

DFollow-up

  • Log every lead, not just signed clients, with one of six statuses: New, Attempted Contact, Unable to Contact, Qualified, Rejected, Retained.
  • Write an "unable to reach" cadence of calls, texts and emails over several days, and template the messages.
  • Create a task with a due date for every lead waiting on documents.

EAttribution and oversight

  • Use tracking numbers per campaign and carry campaign tags on web forms, so every lead arrives with its source.
  • Each quarter, divide spend by signed cases for each source.
  • Review call reports monthly against simple benchmarks: no missed new-case calls, no hold over 30 seconds.
  • Mystery-shop intake every quarter and whenever you hire someone new for intake.

Templates worth writing once

Set these up in your CRM or email tool so nobody writes them from scratch at 9pm.

  • "Thank you for your inquiry": a short series that introduces the firm and reassures the lead
  • The e-sign retainer email
  • "Unable to reach you": an email sequence and a text asking the lead to call
  • The welcome email after the retainer is signed
  • After-hours web-form reply: when they'll hear from a person
  • Missed-call text-back
The PI Intake Scorecard08

Self mystery-shop

Call your own firm

  1. Have someone outside the firm call your main number as a new PI prospect, once during business hours and once after hours. Log both calls below.
  2. If a callback was promised, time how long it took and note the channel (call, text or email).
  3. Submit your own website form and start a chat. Time the first personal response.
  4. Pull last month's call report. Count missed and abandoned calls, and check whether each one got a callback.
  5. Pick five leads from last month that didn't sign. Check their status, how many follow-up attempts they got, and whether you know their source.

Your 30-day plan

One section a week

Work through one section a week, starting with your lowest. Tick each item off as it's done, then re-score at the end of week 4.

Week 1

Measure

  • Owner and intake lead score the scorecard separately
  • Run both mystery-shop calls and the online test (page 9)
  • Pull last month's call report and lead log
  • Agree on the lowest-scoring section and any red flags
Week 2

Close the gaps at the phone

  • Fix after-hours and weekend coverage (question 2)
  • Name an owner for missed calls, with a five-minute target (question 7)
  • Route web-form and chat alerts to a phone
  • Set the "name and number first" rule for everyone who answers
Week 3

Script and follow-up

  • Write or update the intake script, in order
  • Let intake book consults or send e-sign retainers on the call
  • Give every lead a status in the CRM or lead log
  • Write the "unable to reach" cadence and templates
Week 4

Track and re-score

  • Make sure every lead arrives with its source (question 17)
  • Work out last quarter's cost per signed case by source
  • Put a monthly call-report review on the calendar
  • Re-score and compare with week 1
